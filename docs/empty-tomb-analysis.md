# Analysis: Atwill's "Puzzle of the Empty Tomb" (Caesar's Messiah, ch. 6)

*Companion to `menasgotz-aptvs-review.md` and `control-experiment.md`.
Chapter read in full 2026-08-16; all verse citations checked against the
KJV corpus in this instrument.*

## The claim

The four Gospel tomb accounts contradict one another (time of day, number
of women, number and position of "angels"). Atwill proposes they are one
interlocking narrative: order the accounts by the sun's position (John's
"yet dark" → Matthew's dawning → Mark's post-sunrise → Luke), and John's
choreography of Peter and the beloved disciple — one man outside looking
in, then one inside, then two inside — matches the angel encounters of
Matthew (one outside), Mark (one inside), Luke (two inside). The "angels"
are the disciples, mistaken by overwrought mourners; Mary found *Lazarus's*
empty tomb in the dark; no one rose. A 21-factor probability chain (1 in
254,803,968) is offered as proof that one hand wrote all four Gospels.

## What is genuinely there

- The discrepancies are systematic, not random: the four accounts share an
  identical skeleton and diverge only in low-stakes details. Real datum.
- The Lazarus echo is real: John 11 and John 20 share the grave clothes
  and the *soudarion*, and Lazarus's opened tomb prefigures Jesus's.
- The accounts are genuinely composable into a sequence. The question is
  what kind of interconnection that establishes.

## The mechanics under pressure

1. **The cornerstone ordering fails on Luke's Greek.** Atwill needs Luke
   last, after Mark's sunrise; but Luke's *orthrou batheōs* is "deep
   dawn" — naturally *earlier* than Mark's "when the sun had risen." The
   sequence is imposed, not read.
2. **The two tombs are two miles apart.** The comedy needs Lazarus's tomb
   adjacent to Jesus's. John locates Lazarus at Bethany, "about fifteen
   furlongs off" (11:18), over the Mount of Olives; Jesus lies at the
   crucifixion site, "in the garden... nigh at hand" (19:41–42). Mary's
   founding mistake requires wandering to the wrong village across a
   mountain in the dark.
3. **"Simon Peter ≠ Peter" breaks the rest of John.** John 20:2–3 uses the
   names interchangeably for one man, as Johannine style does throughout
   (the foot-washing, Gethsemane). A disambiguation rule that detonates
   every passage except its target is a patch, not a discovery.
4. **The fit survives by an asymmetric rule.** Presences are load-bearing,
   absences excused: Matthew's guards who faint (at a jogging fisherman?),
   Matthew 28:9 where the women meet and grasp Jesus himself (who, in a
   story where no one rose?), Mark's women passing the loitering disciple
   unmentioned, Luke's two men who "stood by them" (24:4) — standing, not
   sitting inside the tomb where the mapping seats them.

## The probability chain

Same machine as the APTVS odds, same three failures:

- **Post-hoc factorization.** The 21 "facts" are the details that fit,
  scored against sample spaces the solution itself defines; the details
  that would zero out the reading are not enumerated.
- **The authors are not independent.** Matthew and Luke used Mark; John
  shares the tradition. Redaction of a common skeleton is the ordinary
  generator of small patterned divergences — and the angel gradient
  (Mark's young man → Matthew's angel → Luke's two men → John's two
  angels) is a textbook redactional escalation. The chain computes odds
  against authors rolling dice; the real null is redaction, under which
  the pattern costs nothing.
- **The experiment has been run for 1,800 years.** Atwill's method is
  structurally identical to Christian harmonization: Tatian's Diatessaron,
  Augustine, and modern harmonies (e.g. Wenham's *Easter Enigma*) sequence
  the same accounts into "perfectly logical" combined stories that prove
  the *opposite* conclusion. One puzzle, multiple incompatible "unique
  solutions" — the coherence comes from the method's flexibility, not
  from a message.

## What explains what he saw

Markan priority plus redaction explains the systematic discrepancies —
which is a real interconnection of the Gospels, by ordinary literary
dependence. The Lazarus/Jesus echo is deliberate parallelism *within John,
by John* — design correctly detected, author misattributed. The evangelists
were sophisticated literary artists reworking sources and each other;
Atwill finds their fingerprints and assigns them to Titus.

## Verdict

The chapter's observation is real, its construction clever, and its
resolution *feels* like decryption. But the ordering contradicts Luke's
time marker, the founding mistake needs a two-mile error, the character
splitting breaks every text outside its target, the "perfect fit" is
maintained by scoring hits and excusing misses, and the probability chain
computes the wrong null with hand-picked factors. What remains is the
mainstream position: the Gospels are interdependent literary documents
whose contradictions are redactional — resolvable in many directions
precisely because they are under-determined.
